Universal Differential Equations for Learning
Wilson–Cowan Neural Population Dynamics
Shafik Houeidi, Mohamad Faour, Suhaib Abu Raidah
1 Motivation and Scientific Question
Understanding how neural populations in the visual cortex collectively encode, transform, and relay
visual information remains one of the central challenges in systems neuroscience. Classical mean-
field models, most notably the Wilson–Cowan (W–C) equations [1], offer a biophysically grounded,
interpretable account of excitatory–inhibitory (E–I) population dynamics, capturing phenomena
such as oscillations, multistability, and stimulus-evoked transients. However, these models rely on
simplifying assumptions (for example homogeneous connectivity and static activation functions)
that cause systematic deviations when confronted with real, multi-area cortical recordings.
On the other end of the spectrum, purely data-driven latent dynamics models, such as latent
ODEs [2] and population-level neural differential equations (PLNDE) [3], achieve high predictive
accuracy but sacrifice the mechanistic interpretability that neuroscientists need to generate falsifi-
able hypotheses. A recent position paper [4] argues that universal differential equations (UDEs) [5]
provide a unifying framework for neuroscience. However, their application to real multi-region
electrophysiology data is relatively limited compared to pure data-driven latent dynamic models.
Scientific question:Can a hybrid mechanistic and machine learning model, grounded in Wilson–
Cowan dynamics and augmented with a learnable neural network residual, discover interpretable
corrections to classical neural population equations that improve prediction and generalization of
real visual cortex dynamics across brain regions and stimulus conditions?
2 Background and Related Work
The Wilson–Cowan model [1] has been the workhorse of population-level neural modeling for over
fifty years, successfully describing cortical oscillations, E–I balance, and spatiotemporal pattern for-
mation [6]. Extensions incorporating homeostatic mechanisms [7], delays, and multi-area coupling
have broadened its applicability but still rely on hand-specified functional forms.
Data-driven approaches such as LFADS, Neural ODEs [2], and PLNDE [3] learn latent dynamics
directly from spiking data but produce latent spaces that lack direct mechanistic interpretability.
The SINDy framework [8] enables sparse recovery of governing equations from data but has mainly
been applied to low-dimensional synthetic systems.
Hybrid UDE approaches have demonstrated the ability to auto-discover missing physics in ecology,
1
pharmacology, and fluid dynamics [5, 9]. A 2025 review explicitly calls for their adoption in
neuroscience [4]. To the best of our knowledge, as of this date, no prior work has proposed a UDE
pipeline combining a mechanistic backbone with learned residuals followed by symbolic distillation
for large-scale Neuropixels recordings from the visual cortex.
Positioning of Our Work:In contrast to prior work, our approach makes the following contri-
butions:
•Hybrid UDE formulation for neural population dynamics:We propose a universal
differential equation framework that embeds the Wilson–Cowan population model within a
neural differential equation. A neural network residual augments the classical dynamics by
learning data-driven corrections while preserving the mechanistic structure of the Wilson–
Cowan equations.
•Interpretable discovery of corrected neural dynamics:After training the hybrid model
on large-scale Neuropixels recordings, we apply symbolic regression to the learned residual
dynamics to extract interpretable modifications to the Wilson–Cowan equations. This allows
improved predictive accuracy while maintaining scientific interpretability.

population dynamics.

4 Dataset
We will use the Allen Brain Observatory Visual Coding Neuropixels dataset, a public collection of
multi region spiking recordings during passive visual stimulation [10, 11]. From the dataset, we will
map spike trains to population level observations using binned spike counts and smoothed firing
rates at multiple bin widths, with stimulus timing treated as an exogenous inputu(t).
